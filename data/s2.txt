This research investigates the rise of AI slop, a term describing low-quality, algorithmically generated content that increasingly permeates online spaces. Though not inherently deceptive, AI slop tends to be vapid, artificial, and lacking in aesthetic merit. The concept, while increasingly prevalent, has received limited academic attention and definition. This study employs mixed methods, combining qualitative thematic analysis and quantitative topic modeling to examine sources cited in the Wikipedia entry on AI slop, supplemented by an efficient large language model approach that achieved similar results. The analysis identified four key themes in discussions of AI slop: productivity versus quality, risk assessment and detection, knowledge integrity, and attention economics. Common characterizations of slop emphasize its deficiencies - describing it as inaccurate, artificial, mediocre, bizarre, warped, formulaic, purposeless, or tacky - often using metaphors of pollution, invasion, or waste. The research categorizes AI slop into four primary variants: social media content, corporate communications, academic submissions, and news articles. Additionally, this work presents the novel SlopNews dataset for analyzing linguistic patterns in AI-generated news content. Analysis reveals that compared to human-written news, slop exhibits reduced complexity, less linguistic diversity, and skews toward positive sentiment. These insights provide a crucial framework for understanding and addressing the challenges posed by AI-generated content of dubious quality. The findings can guide professionals across education, publishing, research, and content moderation in their efforts to manage AI slop and preserve the integrity of online discourse.
