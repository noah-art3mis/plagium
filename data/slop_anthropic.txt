AI slop refers to the flood of low-quality, algorithmically generated content that's increasingly cluttering the internet. It encompasses everything from generic blog posts and articles churned out by language models to mass-produced images, videos, and social media posts created with minimal human oversight or creative input. What makes it "slop" isn't necessarily that AI was involved in its creation, but rather that it's created purely for engagement farming, SEO manipulation, or content mill purposes—optimized for algorithms rather than human value. This deluge of mediocre, often repetitive content is polluting search results, social feeds, and online spaces, making it harder to find authentic, thoughtfully created material. The term captures a growing frustration with how AI tools, which hold genuine potential for creativity and productivity, are instead being deployed en masse to generate forgettable content that adds noise rather than signal to our digital ecosystem.
